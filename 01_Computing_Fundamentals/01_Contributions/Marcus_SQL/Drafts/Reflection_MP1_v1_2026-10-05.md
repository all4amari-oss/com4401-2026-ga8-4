# Individual Reflection: Mini Project 1 (Northwest Architecture Studio)

**Marcus Weeks · Role: Architect / CAD Designer build and SQL database**

## My role and what I did

I owned the Architect / CAD Designer build. I chose and priced every part on BuildCores and wrote a reason for each one. The final build costs £1,844.50 per PC, which is £655.50 under the £2,500 budget, and the studio needs 5 of them. I also built the SQL database, ran the 11 queries from the brief with a screenshot of each, and tested the data integrity rules. Later I helped bring all four builds together into one team database.

## How I learned it

I didn't know about SQL or BuildCores before this assignment. I knew what the individual parts were, but I'd never even thought about building a computer myself. I learned by working through the problems with Claude, and the tutor's examples and slides were a big help. Together we figured it out.

## What was hardest

The hardest part was following the brief on the SQL. One little mistake and it would show an error. Once I realised what the problem was, it was an easy fix.

## What I got wrong and fixed

BuildCores showed the wrong price for my mouse. It was another mouse's price, £17.99, so I changed it to the real Lenovo price of £36.11. I'd also left out the network cable, so I added it at £3.48.

My first database followed the slides instead of the diagram in the brief, so I rebuilt it to match the brief exactly. The team file also had different budgets from the brief, £850 instead of £950 for example, and I went with the brief.

The brief asks for data integrity and I hadn't covered it, so I added it with four tested examples. By the end the team had two separate databases, mine and Nah's, so we merged them into one team database with all four builds.

## What I'll do differently next time

I'll read the brief fully before I start, not just the slides, because that's where my database and budget mistakes came from. I'll check every price and spec against the shop's own page. And I'll agree one database and one budget with the team from the start, so we don't end up with two.
